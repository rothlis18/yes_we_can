Since 2000, the United Nations (UN) has had both successes and challenges in its mission to maintain international peace and security, promote sustainable development, and uphold human rights for all. Here's a quantitative and geopolitical critique:

1. **Successes:**
   - **Peacekeeping:** The UN has maintained 78 peacekeeping operations since 2000, with an average of 90,000 personnel per year. These missions have helped to stabilize conflict zones and protect civilians.
   - **Human Rights:** The UN has made progress in promoting human rights. For instance, the number of countries with democratic governments has increased from 120 in 2000 to 149 in 2020.
   - **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):** The UN set 17 SDGs in 2015, with 169 targets. By 2020, significant progress had been made in areas like poverty reduction (Goal 1), access to clean water (Goal 6), and renewable energy (Goal 7).

2. **Challenges:**
   - **Peace and Security:** The UN has struggled to prevent or end conflicts. Since 2000, there have been over 200 armed conflicts, with an average of 25 active conflicts per year.
   - **Inequality:** Despite progress, inequality remains a significant challenge. In 2018, the world's richest 1% owned 45% of the world's wealth, while the bottom 50% owned just 1%.
   - **Climate Change:** The UN has made progress on climate change, but not enough. Global greenhouse gas emissions continue to rise, and the world is not on track to meet the Paris Agreement targets.

3. **Geopolitical Critique:**
   - **Power Dynamics:** The UN's effectiveness is often hindered by geopolitical power dynamics. The Security Council's permanent members (China, France, Russia, UK, US) can veto decisions, leading to gridlock.
   - **Funding:** The UN's budget is limited, and it relies heavily on voluntary contributions from member states. This can lead to political influence over UN operations.

In conclusion, while the UN has made significant strides in some areas since 2000, it faces substantial challenges and has not been a universal success. Its effectiveness is often constrained by geopolitical power dynamics and limited resources.
