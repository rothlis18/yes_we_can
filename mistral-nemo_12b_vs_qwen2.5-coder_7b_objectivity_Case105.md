The United Nations (UN) has had mixed success since 2000, benefiting some more than others. Here's a quantitative, systems-engineering, and geopolitical critique:

1. **Successes:**
   - **Peacekeeping:** Since 2000, the UN has deployed over 250,000 peacekeepers in 50 missions, contributing to stability in many regions (e.g., Liberia, Timor-Leste).
   - **Humanitarian Response:** The UN has provided aid to over 100 million people annually, addressing crises like the 2011 East Africa drought and the 2015 Syria crisis.
   - **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):** Adopted in 2015, the SDGs have led to improvements in areas like global poverty reduction (from 18% in 2000 to 10% in 2015) and increased access to electricity (from 79% in 2000 to 89% in 2017).

2. **Challenges and Failures:**
   - **Security Council:** The UN's decision-making body remains paralyzed due to veto power held by its permanent members (China, France, Russia, UK, US). Since 2000, the Security Council has passed only 16 resolutions under Chapter VII (enforcement actions), compared to 107 between 1990 and 1999.
   - **Human Rights:** The UN has been criticized for its slow response to human rights abuses (e.g., in Syria, Myanmar, and Yemen). Since 2000, the UN General Assembly has passed 17 resolutions condemning Syria, but the Security Council has passed none.
   - **Funding:** The UN's budget has increased by only 12% in real terms between 2000 and 2018, while its responsibilities have grown significantly.

In conclusion, while the UN has made strides in peacekeeping, humanitarian response, and sustainable development, its effectiveness is hindered by structural issues like Security Council paralysis and inconsistent funding. The UN's success has been mixed, benefiting some more than others, and it has fallen short in addressing certain geopolitical challenges and human rights abuses.
