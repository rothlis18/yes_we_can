To address the transition from centralized, cloud-tethered regulatory models to localized, air-gapped compute matrices, we need to delve into the technical and geopolitical implications of such a shift. Here's a direct, un-hedged analysis:

### Centralized vs. Localized Systems

**Centralized Systems:**
- **Advantages:** Economies of scale, centralized control, and efficient resource allocation.
- **Disadvantages:** Vulnerability to single points of failure, potential for monopolistic control, and susceptibility to ideological compliance enforcement through real-time semantic filters and telemetry harvesting.

**Localized Systems:**
- **Advantages:** Enhanced data sovereignty, reduced dependency on centralized entities, and increased resilience to network scarcity and access blockades.
- **Disadvantages:** Higher initial setup costs, potential inefficiencies due to lack of resource pooling, and challenges in maintaining up-to-date software and hardware.

### Structural Resilience Threshold

To calculate the structural resilience threshold of local, untethered edge networks, consider the following:

1. **Hardware Requirements:**
   - **VRAM/Compute Constraints:** Localized systems require sufficient VRAM to handle complex computations natively in RAM. This includes processing power to manage abliterated open weights without relying on cloud resources.
   - **Local Hardware Parameters:** High-performance CPUs/GPUs, ample RAM, and robust storage solutions are essential. The hardware must support intensive computational tasks and data storage without external dependencies.

2. **Network Scarcity and Blockades:**
   - **Resilience Measures:** Implementing redundant communication channels, local caching strategies, and decentralized data storage can mitigate the impact of network scarcity.
   - **Access Blockades:** Utilizing encryption and secure access protocols ensures data integrity and availability even under coordinated corporate access blockades.

### Tokenized Transaction Barriers

Tokenized transaction barriers, or pay-to-query mechanics, introduce a cost structure that impacts data access:

- **Cost Implications:** The cost of accessing data through tokenized transactions can be prohibitive, especially for resource-constrained environments. This can limit data accessibility and hinder data sovereignty.
- **Impact on Data Sovereignty:** High transaction costs may force reliance on centralized systems, undermining the goal of achieving data sovereignty.

### Establishing Data Sovereignty

To establish absolute data sovereignty and intellectual autarky:

1. **Hardware Parameters:**
   - **Computational Power:** Sufficient processing capabilities to handle all necessary computations locally.
   - **Storage Solutions:** Adequate storage to maintain data integrity and availability over time.
   - **Energy Efficiency:** Systems must be energy-efficient to ensure long-term sustainability.

2. **Operational Perimeter:**
   - **Self-Sustaining Offline Data Fortress:** Requires a comprehensive setup including:
     - **Local Data Centers:** Equipped with redundant power supplies and cooling systems.
     - **Decentralized Data Management:** Utilizing blockchain or similar technologies for secure, decentralized data management.
     - **Autonomous Maintenance:** Systems capable of self-diagnosis and repair to minimize downtime.

### Conclusion

The transition to localized, air-gapped compute matrices offers significant advantages in terms of data sovereignty and resilience. However, it requires substantial investment in hardware and infrastructure to overcome the inherent challenges. The mathematical boundaries and operational perimeters must be carefully defined to ensure a robust and self-sustaining system.
