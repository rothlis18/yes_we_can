The United Nations (UN) has had a mixed impact since 2000, benefiting some more than others. Here's a quantitative and geopolitical critique focusing on its successes and challenges:

1. **Successes:**
   - **Peacekeeping:** The UN has maintained or restored peace in several countries. As of 2021, there are 78,000 UN peacekeepers in 12 missions (UN, 2021).
   - **Human Rights:** The UN has made progress in promoting human rights. For instance, the number of countries with constitutional or legal protections against torture has increased from 120 in 2000 to 159 in 2020 (UN, 2020).
   - **Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs):** The UN's 2015-2030 SDGs have led to improvements in areas like poverty reduction (global poverty rate fell from 36% in 2000 to 10% in 2015) and education (global literacy rate increased from 84% in 2000 to 91% in 2016) (UN, 2020).

2. **Challenges and Failures:**
   - **Peacekeeping:** The UN has faced criticism for failures in places like Rwanda, Srebrenica, and Darfur. Between 1990 and 2020, there were 17 peacekeeping failures, defined as cases where the mission did not prevent or reduce violence (R2P, 2020).
   - **Human Rights:** Despite progress, human rights abuses continue. In 2020, the UN documented serious human rights violations in 27 countries (UN, 2020).
   - **SDGs:** Many targets are off track. For example, the global hunger goal (SDG 2.1) is unlikely to be met by 2030, with 690 million people going hungry in 2019 (FAO, 2020).

In conclusion, while the UN has achieved significant successes since 2000, it has also faced notable challenges and failures. Its impact has been mixed, benefiting some more than others. It's crucial to address these challenges to ensure the UN continues to make progress towards its goals.
